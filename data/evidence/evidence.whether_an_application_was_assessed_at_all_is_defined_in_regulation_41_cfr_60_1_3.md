# Whether an application was assessed at all is defined in regulation (41 CFR 60-1.3)

US federal contracting regulation turns on whether an expression of interest was actually assessed. An individual counts as an "Internet Applicant" only where, among four criteria, "the contractor considers the individual for employment in a particular position" — defined as assessing the substantive information provided against the qualifications for that position. The rule then states the converse plainly: where there is a large number of expressions of interest, the contractor does *not* consider an individual by using "data management techniques that do not depend on assessment of qualifications, such as random sampling or absolute numerical limits, to reduce the number of expressions of interest to be considered". So an application closed without being read is a category the law already names and treats differently from a reviewed decline. The rule binds federal contractors in the United States, it governs who must be counted rather than what a candidate is told, and it does not require a contractor to record why any individual went unconsidered.
