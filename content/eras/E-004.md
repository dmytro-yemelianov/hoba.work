# A fixed number of seats

Two official series say most of it, and they say it together. Gross hires in the
US information sector fell 28.9% between 2022 and 2025. Layoffs and discharges
in the same official series barely moved over those years. The sector shrank
because people were not hired to replace the people who left, not because people
were removed at an accelerating rate — which is why a tracker of announced cuts
and the official separations series tell such different stories about the same
period.

That distinction is the whole point of this page. A separations problem gets
better when the announcements stop. A hiring problem does not: it gets better
when seats open. Seats open when incumbents leave, and the information-sector
quits rate is at 1.1%, roughly half what it was in June 2022.

So the arithmetic a candidate is inside: fewer openings, more applications per
opening, and the openings that exist described at senior level. That is
competition for a fixed and shrinking number of seats. It is not a statement
about anyone's ability, and it is not a claim that any employer behaved badly —
it is what happens to a queue when the queue stops moving.

The registry documents what that produces at each gate. This page exists so that
those entries are not mistaken for a description of the candidate.
